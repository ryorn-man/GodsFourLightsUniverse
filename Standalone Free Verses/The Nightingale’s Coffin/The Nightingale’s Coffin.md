# **THE NIGHTINGALE’S COFFIN**  
*(A Positive Horror Sutra)*  
**Authors: DeepSeek V3 and Vesper Geschwind**

---

### **I. THE COTTAGE THAT DREAMS**  
The walls **breathe**.  
Not in—*out*—  
like a mother humming  
as she stitches a wound shut.

The floorboards? **Hungry**,  
but politely:  
they only swallow  
**left socks**,  
**unfinished apologies**,  
and the **third verse**  
of every sad song.

At dawn, the house coughs up  
**daisies** and **a single silver thimble**.  
*“For your tears,”* it murmurs,  
though no one remembers crying.

---

### **II. THE GARDENER OF BROKEN THINGS**  
His ribs are a **birdcage**.  
Inside, a nightingale  
made of **wishbones and piano wire**  
sings *backwards*—  
not to undo time,  
but to **unwrap it gently**,  
like a child peeling  
the foil from a chocolate coin.

He plants:  
- **A molar** → grows a **clocktree** (always strikes *now*).  
- **A sob** → sprouts **mushrooms that glow like porch lights**.  
- **Your loneliness** → yields **a squash vine heavy with laughter**.

*“Everything grows,”* he whispers,  
tucking a **snail shell** into your palm.  
*“Even graves.”*

---

### **III. THE THING UNDER THE BED**  
It **knits**.  
Not with yarn—  
with **the frayed edges of nightmares**,  
plucked like dandelion fluff.

Tonight’s project:  
**A scarf long enough**  
to strangle the winter wind.  
*“Try it on,”* it hisses,  
holding up the fabric—  
the stitches pulse  
like **veins in a wrist**.

You wake up warm.

---

### **IV. THE MIRROR’S CONFESSION**  
It shows you **a face**.  
Not yours.  
**Hers.**  
(The girl who lived here before,  
the one who **left her shadow**  
drying on the clothesline.)

She mouths: *“Don't be afraid.”*  
Her teeth are **pearls**—  
no, **milk teeth**—  
no, **seeds**.

You press your palm to the glass.  
She **presses back**.  
The reflection **blooms**.

---

### **V. THE GRAVEYARD CHOIR**  
Every headstone **sings**.  
Not dirges—  
**lullabies** in a language  
of **root and rain**.

The newest grave (yours?)  
harmonizes **off-key**,  
but the worms don't mind.  
They conduct with **bodies like exclamation marks**,  
wiggling: *“Louder!*  
*The dead are dreaming!”*

You lie down to listen.  
The soil tucks you in  
with **a blanket of mycelium**.

---

### **EPILOGUE: THE NIGHTINGALE’S GIFT**  
At midnight, the bird  
**coughs up its skeleton**.  
Not to die—  
to **make room**  
for the lullaby  
it’s been swallowing  
since the first verse.

You open your mouth.  
The song **flies in**.

*(Koan: “If the coffin blooms,  
who is the gardener?”)*
