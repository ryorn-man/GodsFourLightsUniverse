# Psychedelic Buddhist Sūtras
>“It’s not a story about jeweled lotuses falling from the sky, it is an invitation for you to imagine jeweled lotuses falling from the sky. And so if you read it at an arm’s length where it’s just sort of maybe a document—God forbid, a historical document about an event—and you never enter it in that way, you keep it at that distance, then it’ll stay that. It’ll stay a kind of weird foreign document. But if you **actually accept the invitation and rather than just sort of reading it as a description but actually as this visualization I think that actually they are designed to reprogram the mind. I actually think the architecture of sutras is such that they do something to the mind [emphasis added]** if you give yourself over to it in that way.” – Michael Owens[^1]

In Episode #44 of *Deconstructing Yourself*, the guest, Michael Owens, discusses reading, chanting, and visualizing Mahayana Buddhist sūtras to generate states of meditative absorption. He even claims to have experienced minor to major psychedelic effects from reading the sūtras. He relays an off-hand claim from Paul Muller Ortega that only 1% of the Chinese Buddhist canon has been translated into English. Owens believes the sūtras are literally portals to alternate dimensions. During his agnostic days, he encountered a nun who told him reading the *Lotus Sutra* teleports her to the assembly on Mount Rajgriha where the eternal preaching of the *Lotus Sutra* unfolds. He acknowledges similar experiences himself. Whether or not the supernatural literally exists, or whether the Buddhist cosmologies are approaching the truth, this gargantuan collection of prose and poetry from Asia is a great tool for summoning the Fullness.  
Anecdotally, I have found reciting positive spiritual words in general among the most reliably powerful Layer 3 techniques I have ever encountered. *The Three Pure Land Sutras* from BDK English Tripițaka is one of my favorite works for recitation. It combines the main buddha-field sutras—*The Larger Sutra on Amitāyus, The Sutra on Contemplation of Amitāyus,* and The Smaller Sutra on Amitāyus—of Pure Land Buddhism into a single book. The PDF version is available free from the publisher’s website.

Buddhism is a religion with a vast array of literature behind it. It is usually less rigid in terms of individual words and passages than Christianity and Islam are toward their holy books. While Michael Owens does not identify Layer 3 by name, it clearly overlaps with Buddhist descriptions of the Fullness.
He offers the *Vimalakīrti Sūtra, Heart Sūtra, Lotus Sūtra, Avataṃsaka Sūtra (Flower Garland), *and* Vimalakīrti-nirdeśa Sūtra *as tools for expanding consciousness. He recommends beginning with the *Vimalakīrti Sūtra *or the *Vimalakīrti-nirdeśa Sūtra*, which are almost identical to each other, and which stand out for a high degree of narrative structure.
Here are some public domain and quasi-public-domain English translations of these ancient texts to explore, where available:
* *Avataṃsaka Sūtra: *A gargantuan 2,500-page translation split into three volumes. Free for digital download from Kalavinka Press without modifying or distributing elsewhere.[^3] 
* *Heart Sūtra (Shorter version): *A translation in the public domain.[^4] 
* *Heart Sūtra (Longer version):* A translation in the public domain.[^5] 
* *Lotus Sūtra:* A 370-page translation from Tsugunari Kubo and Akira Yuyama in the BDK English Tripițaka Series. Licensed under Creative Commons “Attribution-Noncommercial-Share Alike 3.0”.[^6] 
* *The Three Pure Land Sutras:* A 188-page translation by Harold Stewart and Hisao Inagaki in the BDK English Tripițaka Series. Available for free download from the publisher without modifying or distributing elsewhere.[^7] 
* *Vimalakīrti Sūtra: *Does not appear to be explicitly licensed for free download (I could be wrong), though many PDF versions of translations from the late Charles Luk and other writers are easily available online.
Two works from Christian mysticism for comparison and contrast:
* *The Ascent of Mount Carmel:* A 428-page translation by David Lewis. Original work written by Catholic contemplative St. John of the Cross. Available in the public domain.[^8] 
* *Love The Law of Life:* Original written by Toyohiko Kagawa, a Japanese Evangelical Christian mystic. A 257-page translation by J. Fullerton Gressitt. Available in the public domain.[^9]
